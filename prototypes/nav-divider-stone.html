Drag the height slider — that is the whole test. The rail is height:100vh, so this is exactly what changing browser height does. Watch what each option does to the bottom of the shaft where it meets the lower capital.

The supplied strip is not a designed tile. Its mortar lines sit at rows 106, 215, 350 and 567, so the courses run 109, 135 and ~108px — irregular, which is what AI art does. Wrapping the full strip end to end costs 11.9 mean per-channel error at the splice. Cutting one course by hand at the mortar centres scores 40.3 — worse than not tiling. The tile in C and D was found by searching every offset and height from 95–150px for the pair of rows that actually match: 7.4 at y=330, h=120px.

Native width is 66px, and that caps quality. Displayed above ~33px you are under 2× on a HiDPI screen and the stone will soften. If you want it wider than that, regenerate the art larger rather than upscaling. The three PNGs are ~190KB together; WebP at quality 88 would be a fraction of that, and scripts/trim-image.py already does exactly that conversion.

Width cost, again. At 26px this is 11% of the expanded rail and 36% of the collapsed one. Tick collapsed rail to judge it — that is the in-game state.
