Drag the row-height slider — that is what the cap being a slice buys you. The cap's bright rim holds at 4px top and bottom at any height, because only its middle stretches. A plain background-size:100% 100% would smear that rim thicker as the row grows.

Then drag the width. C's cap stays a constant 19px pinned right, and the label truncates into it — same as A. B's cap does too, but B's whole plaque is raster, so its navy cannot follow the theme and its rim cannot be tuned independently of the art.

Collapsed is where C earns it. A drops the cap and reads as a clean tile but loses the artwork entirely; B keeps the cap and drops it on top of the icon; C drops the cap and keeps the CSS plaque, so the collapsed rail looks deliberate and the expanded one keeps the real gold.

The chevron and the cap coexist. Play Chess is active AND has a submenu, and the chevron simply sits inboard of the cap because the padding reserves its width. Tight, not broken.

The rim is structural. The navy is only 1.03:1 against the rail in luminance — the plaque separates by hue and by that bright top/bottom rim, not by lightness. In C the rim is CSS, so it is tunable; keep it bright or the active state stops reading in greyscale and forced-colors mode. Text is comfortable either way (TEXT_PRIMARY 12.2:1, ACCENT_BRIGHT 8.3:1).
